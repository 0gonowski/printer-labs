How to save WebP and AVIF images as PNG or JPG in Chrome

Chrome's “Save image as…” keeps the format the website sends, and more and more sites send WebP or AVIF because the files are smaller. Many apps, older editors and upload forms still refuse those formats. Here are three ways to end up with a PNG or JPG.

Updated 27 November 2026. Written by Printer Labs, the developer of PicShift, the third option below.

1. Convert the file after saving it

  • Windows: open the saved file in Paint or Photos and choose Save as, then PNG or JPEG. If Windows cannot open it, install Microsoft's free “WebP Image Extensions” (for WebP) or “AV1 Video Extension” (for AVIF) from the Microsoft Store.
  • Mac: open the file in Preview, choose File, Export, then PNG or JPEG.

Good for an image now and then; slow when you collect many.

2. Use an online converter

Upload the file, pick the format, download the result. It works anywhere, but the image goes to someone else's server, so keep private pictures out of it.

3. Save it as PNG or JPG from the right-click menu

PicShift adds a PicShift submenu to Chrome's right-click menu on images:

  1. Right-click the image.
  2. Open PicShift and choose PNG, JPG or WebP.

The picture is converted on your computer at its original pixel size and saved in the format you chose. “Copy image as PNG” puts it on the clipboard for pasting into Word, PowerPoint, Photoshop or a chat. For a whole page, click the PicShift icon in the toolbar to see every image with its size and save a selection at once.

Questions

PNG or JPG?

PNG keeps every pixel and supports transparency, which suits screenshots, logos and graphics. JPG makes much smaller files for photos. When a transparent image becomes a JPG, the transparent areas need a background colour; PicShift lets you choose it.

Does converting reduce quality?

Saving as PNG loses nothing. JPG and WebP are compressed formats; PicShift lets you set their quality.

Why does Chrome download WebP when the picture looked like any other?

Browsers display WebP and AVIF like any other image. Sites use them because pages load faster; the format only shows once you save the file.
