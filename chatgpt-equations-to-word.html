How to get ChatGPT and Gemini equations into Word as real, editable equations

Copy an answer with math from ChatGPT or Gemini into Word and the formulas arrive as LaTeX source: backslashes, braces and dollar signs instead of fractions and integrals. Here are three ways to end up with native Word equations you can click and edit.

Updated 27 November 2026. By Tabfinch, the maker of DocShift (method 3).

Why the formulas break

Chat assistants write math in LaTeX and draw it on the page with a rendering library. Copying takes either the drawn result, which Word sees as loose characters, or the LaTeX source, which Word shows as plain text. Word has its own equation format (OMML), so something has to convert LaTeX into it.

1. One formula at a time, with Word's LaTeX input

  1. Ask the assistant for the LaTeX of the formula, or copy it from the answer.
  2. In Word, press Alt+= to insert an equation.
  3. On the Equation tab, switch the input format to LaTeX.
  4. Paste the formula without the surrounding $, \( or \[ delimiters.
  5. Choose Convert, then Professional.

Free and built in, but slow for an answer with many formulas, and tables and headings still need fixing by hand.

2. A whole answer, with pandoc

Save the copied answer as answer.md and run:

pandoc -f markdown+tex_math_single_backslash answer.md -o answer.docx

The option makes pandoc read ChatGPT's \( \) and \[ \] as math, so the .docx gets real Word equations. It needs pandoc installed and a terminal, and you have to copy the answer as Markdown first. The same works for answers from Claude.

3. The whole chat, in one click

DocShift adds an Export button to chatgpt.com and gemini.google.com. You choose the whole chat, only the answers, or the messages you tick, and get a .docx in which:

  • every formula is a native Word equation: fractions, integrals, sums, matrices, aligned steps;
  • tables are real tables, lists keep their nesting, headings use Word's heading styles;
  • code keeps its spacing in a monospace font.

The chat is converted in your browser; nothing is uploaded. PDF, Markdown, HTML and plain text are there too. Five exports a week are free, equations included; Pro is $2.99 a month or $19.99 once.

Questions

Can I still edit the equations later?

Yes. With all three methods the result is Word's own equation format: double-click a formula to change it, as if you had typed it in Word.

What about Google Docs?

Open the .docx in Google Docs: the equations come across as Google Docs equations.

Does the PDF keep the formulas sharp?

Yes. Save the Word file as PDF, or export PDF from DocShift directly; the formulas are text, not pictures.
